At least 12 member states missed the deadline to appoint competent authorities, creating fragmented enforcement risk across the bloc.

The enforcement patchwork across member states creates regulatory arbitrage opportunities but also legal uncertainty.    → *Thesis implication: Regulatory clarity is a net positive for institutional-grade crypto infrastructure plays (custody, compliance tooling, exchange infrastructure). The FCA is also rolling out a roadmap for Open Finance, extending Open Banking-style data sharing to broader areas of consumers' financial lives, with an emphasis on SME lending.
   → *Thesis implication: Significant greenfield opportunity in stablecoin infrastructure and Open Finance data layers.    → *Thesis implication: Third-party risk management and operational resilience tooling becomes a compliance necessity for all FCA-regulated firms. 🔵 **Consumer Credit / Lending / Open Data**: The FCA is consulting on requiring lenders to share borrower credit data with all designated consumer credit reference agencies (CRAs), not just one. Currently, patchy single-CRA reporting creates gaps in credit histories that distort lending decisions and disadvantage consumers with positive repayment histories not fully reflected across agencies.
   → *Thesis implication: Mandatory full-spectrum credit data sharing would enhance underwriting accuracy for alternative lenders and embedded credit platforms. Tailwind for fintechs using multi-bureau data models; incumbent lenders with single-CRA arrangements face compliance remediation costs. Credit infrastructure and data aggregation plays are strategically attractive.*

🔵 **Fintech / Consumer Finance / Challenger Banks**: The FCA's Consumer Duty is shifting from implementation phase to active enforcement in 2026, with multi-firm reviews and data-driven supervision targeting digital lenders, investment apps, and challenger banks. Firms must now evidence that they are actively monitoring customer outcomes and taking corrective action — not merely documenting policies.
   → *Thesis implication: Consumer Duty enforcement raises the compliance bar materially for growth-stage fintechs. Increases operational costs but also rewards firms with strong outcome-monitoring infrastructure. Due diligence must now include Consumer Duty audit readiness as a standard screen; portfolio companies without embedded outcome-tracking tooling face regulatory and reputational exposure.*

### AI Model Updates

🔵 **AI/ML in Healthcare / Clinical AI / LLMs in Medicine**: FDA Commissioner Makary has previewed a forthcoming risk-based AI regulatory framework, describing a three-pronged approach: (1) deregulating low-risk products, (2) shifting emphasis to post-market surveillance leveraging real-world data, and (3) creating forward-looking guidance for anticipated AI products. He also signaled plans to eliminate at least half of existing software/digital health guidance documents to make them 'more clear, more concise, more modern.' The framework is expected to specifically address large language models (LLMs) in healthcare tools within 1–2 years.
   → *Thesis implication: A formal risk-based AI framework would create the 'clear lanes' that enterprise clinical AI buyers have been waiting for, accelerating hospital and payer procurement decisions. LLM-based clinical tools (ambient documentation, AI scribes, clinical Q&A) remain in a regulatory gray zone until the framework lands — creating a finite window for first-mover investment before regulatory clarity raises valuations. Watch for the finalized AI Lifecycle Management guidance as a leading indicator of framework direction.*

🔵 **AI / Financial Crime / RegTech**: The FCA has contracted Palantir to access sensitive UK financial data for AI-driven fraud detection, in a deal worth over £30,000/week. Critics and legal experts have raised concerns about AI model manipulation risks, with one partner warning that bad actors could attempt to influence an AI-based detection system. This signals the FCA's pivot toward AI-powered supervision.
   → *Thesis implication: The FCA's adoption of AI surveillance tools validates the RegTech AI market but raises adversarial risk concerns that could accelerate demand for explainable AI and model integrity solutions. Portfolio companies in fraud detection, AML AI, and model governance stand to benefit from both regulatory demand and enterprise risk management budgets.*
